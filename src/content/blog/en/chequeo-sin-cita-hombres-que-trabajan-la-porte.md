Call him Julio, though he could go by any name. He leaves the house at four thirty, takes 225 with a coffee in the cup holder and badges into the plant before sunrise. He works a twelve-hour rotation: four days on, a few days off. Those days off are not rest. There is a kid's game to get to, brakes to change on the truck, a yard to mow and sleep to catch up on. When someone at home asks when he last got checked, Julio gives the same answer half the crew gives: "I'm fine, just tired."

This piece is not here to lecture Julio. It is here to show him, with real numbers and real hours, that getting checked does not cost him a day's pay, and that some things are not worth pushing back to the next long break.

## What gets said in the break room, and what is worth knowing

Almost every man working at the refineries, the port or on construction sites around here has a reason not to go. None of them is silly, but most of them have a gap.

| What you hear | What is really going on |
| --- | --- |
| "If nothing hurts, nothing's wrong" | High blood pressure, high blood sugar and high cholesterol rarely hurt early on. They show up with a cuff and a blood sample. |
| "I don't have time, I work sunup to sundown" | The clinic is open Monday to Saturday until 9 at night and Sunday until 7. It fits before or after a shift. |
| "They'll just tell me what I already know" | Plenty of men carry an out-of-range number for years that nobody explained. Knowing which one and what to do about it changes the plan. |
| "Without insurance it'll cost a fortune" | No insurance needed. You are told what each test costs before the sample is drawn, so paying holds no surprises. |
| "Some things are embarrassing to bring up" | The visit is private and in Spanish. What you share stays between you and the medical team. |

## Body warnings that should not wait for vacation

Not all tiredness comes from the shift. A few things deserve a visit soon, even if you keep going to work:

- You wake up as worn out as when you lay down, week after week, even when you get your hours.
- You drink water all day and still have a dry mouth, you hit the bathroom more than usual or you have dropped weight without trying.
- The company nurse or your DOT physical flagged high blood pressure and no one ever rechecked it.
- Your urine stream got weaker, you feel like you never quite finish or it is hard to get started.
- Getting or keeping an erection has become harder. Besides being a nuisance, it is sometimes the first hint that circulation or blood sugar is off.
- Your partner says you snore loudly and sometimes stop breathing, and during the day you nod off behind the wheel.
- You are short-tempered, have no drive for anything, and even the weekend does not lift your mood.

None of these signs is a diagnosis. They are reasons to have someone look at you calmly and tell you what is going on.

## What one walk-in visit can cover

The point of the [men's health](/en/services/salud-hombre) visit is to put into one morning what usually gets scattered across three appointments. You walk in with no appointment, you are seen in Spanish or English, and the medical team at Clínica Hispana Nueva Salud La Porte decides with you what makes sense to check based on your age, your symptoms and your family history:

- Blood pressure, weight and waist size, which say a lot about heart risk.
- [Blood work](/en/services/examenes-sangre) with glucose and A1C (your average blood sugar over recent months), cholesterol and triglycerides, plus how your kidneys and liver are working.
- Prostate-specific antigen (PSA), a conversation recommended from age 50, or from 45 if your dad or a brother had prostate cancer.
- A blood testosterone level when there is constant fatigue, lower drive or changes in erections, after first looking at causes like weight, blood sugar, sleep apnea or alcohol.
- A urine test if urinating is uncomfortable or your stream has changed.
- An [electrocardiogram](/en/services/electrocardiograma) when there is high blood pressure, palpitations or heart attacks in the family.

If something comes back out of range, you hear what that number means for you, not just that it is printed in red. When needed, the plan includes specialist referral when results call for it, and you are told the next step clearly.

## How to show up ready so the trip counts

A well-prepared visit saves you from coming back to redo a sample.

**Fasting.** Glucose, cholesterol and triglycerides call for 8 hours without food; water is fine. If you are coming off a night shift, use that: skip the stop at the gas station for a taco or an energy drink, get to the clinic when it opens at 9 and eat breakfast afterward. If you work days, a Sunday morning works too.

**If PSA is on the list.** For the two days before, avoid ejaculation, cycling and heavy exercise, because they can raise the result when there is no real problem.

**Timing.** For a testosterone level, an early sample between 9 and 10 in the morning is best, when the level runs highest.

**What to bring.** Make a list on your phone before leaving the house:

1. The medications, vitamins or supplements you take, with the name as it appears on the box.
2. The sheet from your company physical, your DOT exam or any earlier lab.
3. Your family history: diabetes, blood pressure, heart attacks, prostate cancer.
4. Your questions, however odd they seem. Write them down, because they slip away once you are sitting in the exam room.

## Following up without asking the plant for a day off

What makes a man drop a treatment halfway is rarely the treatment itself. It is having to miss work for every follow-up. That is why it pays to plan your follow-up around your rotation from the very first visit.

With hours Monday to Saturday from 9 in the morning to 9 at night and Sunday from 9 to 7, you can come back to go over your results on your first day off, or on an evening after work if your shift lets out early. If blood pressure, sugar or cholesterol need watching, that care continues under [chronic conditions](/en/services/condiciones-cronicas), and visits get arranged around your weeks off, not the other way around.

And if the medical team prescribes something, there is no extra stop before you head home to sleep: the clinic has a [pharmacy](/en/services/farmacia) in the same building, so you pick up what was prescribed right there and get it explained in Spanish, including how to take it when your schedule flips every week.

## Not a clinic matter: call 911

Some situations make driving to the clinic a waste of precious time. Dial 911 or go to the emergency room if you or someone on the crew:

- Feels something squeezing the chest, with a cold sweat or pain running down the arm or up into the jaw.
- Suddenly cannot catch his breath, gets very dizzy or passes out.
- All at once cannot move one side of the body well or his words come out jumbled.
- Has had a full, aching bladder for hours and cannot pass any urine.
- Has sharp, sudden pain in a testicle that will not ease up.
- Is burning with fever and chills and it also stings to urinate.
